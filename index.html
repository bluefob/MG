Seizure & Recovery Powers (PACE 1984)

Use this reference to determine your lawful authority to recover CCTV evidence when faced with resistance in the field.

1. Voluntary Consent (Normal Route) ▼

Scenario: The owner/manager allows you entry and consents to the download.

Action: Proceed with a standard recovery using the Capture Form. Ensure the owner is aware of what footage is being taken and what it is for.

"If a person consents to a search or to the seizure of property, an officer must ensure the consent is informed and given voluntarily."
2. Refusal of Entry (The Closed Door) ▼

Scenario: You are standing outside a premises (e.g. a locked shop or private home) and the owner refuses to let you inside to view or secure the CCTV.

Power: You generally cannot force entry solely to secure CCTV unless you have a warrant or are executing an arrest.

Action:

  • Do not force entry.
  • Serve a formal preservation request (often verbal initially, followed by written) reminding them of the offence of Perverting the Course of Justice.
  • Consider applying for a Section 8 PACE 1984 Search Warrant via a magistrate.
3. Refusal to Download (The Hostile Owner) ▼

Scenario: You are already lawfully inside a premises (e.g. a shop open to the public, or you were invited in), you see the CCTV DVR, but the owner refuses to provide the password or refuses to let you download the footage.

Power: Section 19 PACE 1984 (General Power of Seizure)

Action: Because you are lawfully on the premises, you may physically seize the entire DVR box if you have reasonable grounds to believe:

  • It contains evidence of an offence.
  • Seizure is necessary to prevent the evidence being Concealed, Lost, Altered, or Destroyed (CLAD).

Practical Tip: Explaining to the owner that you have the lawful power to seize their hardware unit under S.19 PACE usually persuades them to log in and allow a USB export instead.

4. Consent Withdrawn Mid-Recovery ▼

Scenario: You are lawfully on premises downloading footage. Halfway through, the owner panics, tells you to get out, and tries to stop the download.

Power: Section 19 PACE 1984

Action: The moment they withdraw consent and attempt to stop you, the risk of the evidence being "Lost, Altered, or Destroyed" becomes immediate.

  • You no longer rely on consent.
  • You immediately transition to your Section 19 PACE powers.
  • You may remain on the premises specifically to secure the evidence, or physically seize the hard drive/DVR to prevent its destruction.
5. Bulk Data Seizure (Too Much Data) ▼

Scenario: You locate a highly complex CCTV network or massive DVR, but the sheer volume of data makes it impossible or impractical to extract the specific footage at the scene.

Power: Section 50 of the Criminal Justice and Police Act 2001 (CJPA)

Action: This power allows you to physically seize and remove the property from the premises so you can sift through and examine the data elsewhere. You must serve a written notice when exercising this power.

6. Arrested Suspect's Premises ▼

Scenario: A suspect has been arrested for an indictable offence, and you need to secure the CCTV from their home or business address.

Power: Section 18 PACE 1984

Action: You may enter and search any premises occupied or controlled by the arrested person without a warrant. You must have reasonable grounds for suspecting that evidence relating to the offence is on the premises.

7. Cloud or Remote Server Storage ▼

Scenario: The physical camera is on the premises, but the footage is stored on a remote server or in the cloud rather than a local hard drive.

Power: Sections 19(4) and 20 PACE 1984

Action: Section 20 extends seizure powers to computerised information. Under Section 19(4), if you have reasonable grounds to believe the electronic data is evidence of an offence, you can legally require the occupier to produce that information in a visible, legible form that can be taken away.

8. The Pre-Planned Raid (Judicial Warrant) ▼

Scenario: You anticipate that an owner will refuse entry and hide or delete crucial CCTV footage before you can secure it.

Power: Section 8 PACE 1984

Action: You must apply to a magistrate or judge for a warrant. You must demonstrate reasonable grounds for believing an indictable offence occurred and that the premises contain material of substantial value to the investigation.

9. Protected or Privileged Environments ▼

Scenario: You are attempting to seize CCTV from a solicitor's office, a medical clinic, or another environment containing highly confidential files.

Power: Section 19(6) PACE 1984 / Legal Professional Privilege (LPP)

Action: You cannot legally seize material that you have reasonable grounds to believe is subject to legal privilege, such as private medical or legal documentation.
